WHAT IS ICT?

ICT stands for Inner Circle Trader — a trading methodology created by Michael J. Huddleston. The core philosophy is that retail traders consistently lose because they trade against institutional order flow. ICT teaches you to read the market the way institutions actually move it.

THE BUILDING BLOCKS

The eight most essential ICT concepts every student of the methodology needs to understand before applying them to live markets.

01
Foundation

Market Structure

Institutional traders always trade in the direction of the dominant structure. Understanding how price creates swing highs and lows is the lens through which every ICT concept is applied.

  • Higher Highs (HH) + Higher Lows (HL) = Bullish structure
  • Lower Highs (LH) + Lower Lows (LL) = Bearish structure
  • Break of Structure (BOS) = continuation of trend
  • Change of Character (CHOCH) = potential trend reversal
  • Always identify structure on HTF before executing on LTF
02
Key Concept

Order Blocks (OB)

An Order Block is the last bullish or bearish candle before a significant move away from a price level. Institutions leave unfilled orders at these zones — price often returns to them.

  • Bullish OB: last bearish candle before a strong bullish move up
  • Bearish OB: last bullish candle before a strong bearish move down
  • High probability entry when price revisits the OB zone
  • Combine with FVG or liquidity sweep for best results
03
Price Action

Fair Value Gaps (FVG)

A Fair Value Gap is a three-candle formation where the middle candle moves so aggressively that it leaves an imbalance — a gap between the first and third candle's wicks. Price tends to retrace back into FVGs to rebalance.

  • Bullish FVG: gap between candle 1 high and candle 3 low
  • Bearish FVG: gap between candle 1 low and candle 3 high
  • Price "fills" FVGs before continuing in the original direction
  • Also called an "imbalance" or "inefficiency"
04
Liquidity

Liquidity Sweeps

Institutions need massive volume to fill their orders. They deliberately push price above obvious highs or below obvious lows — where retail stop losses cluster — to grab liquidity before reversing.

  • Equal highs / equal lows = liquidity pools (easy targets)
  • Stop hunt above swing high = bearish reversal signal
  • Stop hunt below swing low = bullish reversal signal
  • Never chase the sweep — wait for the reversal confirmation
  • Previous session highs/lows are major liquidity targets
05
Timing

Kill Zones

ICT identifies specific times of day when institutional activity is highest and price is most likely to make its significant daily move. Trading outside kill zones dramatically reduces probability.

  • London Kill Zone: 2:00–5:00 AM EST
  • New York Kill Zone: 8:30–11:00 AM EST (highest volume)
  • London Close: 10:00 AM–12:00 PM EST
  • Avoid: 12:00–2:00 PM EST (lunch — low volume, choppy)
06
Reference Point

Optimal Trade Entry (OTE)

The OTE is a Fibonacci-based entry zone that ICT uses to identify the highest probability point to enter a trade within a pullback. It sits between the 62%–79% retracement of the previous swing.

  • Draw Fibonacci from swing low to swing high (bullish)
  • OTE zone: 0.62 to 0.79 retracement
  • Best entries are inside this zone during kill zones
  • Invalidated if price closes beyond the origin point
07
Daily Bias

Power of 3 (PO3)

Also called "AMD" — Accumulation, Manipulation, Distribution. ICT theorizes that each trading day follows a 3-phase pattern engineered by institutions to trap retail traders before making the real move.

  • Accumulation: institutions quietly build positions overnight
  • Manipulation: false move opposite to intended direction at open
  • Distribution: the real directional move of the day
  • Understanding PO3 helps you avoid stop hunts at the open
08
Reference Level

Previous Day High/Low (PDH/PDL)

The previous day's high and low are among the most important reference levels in ICT. Institutions consistently target these levels for liquidity — price sweeps them and reverses, or uses them as support/resistance.

  • PDH sweep + bearish OB = high probability short
  • PDL sweep + bullish OB = high probability long
  • Mark PDH and PDL every morning before the session opens
  • Also watch: Previous Week High/Low (PWH/PWL)

ICT vs TRADITIONAL

How ICT thinking differs from conventional technical analysis.

Traditional TA
  • Indicators like RSI, MACD, Bollinger Bands✕
  • Buy breakouts above resistance✕
  • Stop loss below support✕
  • Trade at any time of day✕
  • Candlestick pattern signals✕
ICT Methodology
  • Price action only — no indicators needed✓
  • Enter after liquidity sweep at OB or FVG✓
  • Stop below order block (institutional level)✓
  • Only trade during kill zones (high probability)✓
  • Understand who is on the other side of your trade✓

WHERE TO START

ICT has made his full mentorship available for free. Here is the recommended study order.

01
Start Here

Watch ICT's 2022 Mentorship on YouTube. Free. Comprehensive. Start from the beginning and take notes on every concept.

02
Study Price

Spend 60 days backtesting. Mark OBs, FVGs, and liquidity levels on historical charts without trading real money. Pattern recognition takes time.

03
Paper Trade

Apply concepts in a demo account during kill zones only. Track every trade in a journal. Do not go live until consistently profitable for 30+ days.

04
Go Live Small

Start with micro contracts only. 1 MES or 1 MNQ. Prove consistency with real money before scaling. The psychology of real money is different.
